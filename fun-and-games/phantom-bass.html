Phantom Bass

Your ear hears a bass note even when the note's own frequency is missing. Two experiments show how, and how small speakers use it.

Start quiet. The volume slider at the bottom starts low and every tone fades in and out. Use headphones or proper speakers at a moderate level. The Harmonics slider beside it sets every partial above the fundamental louder or quieter against the fundamental, in both parts. A phone speaker hides the low notes, which is the point of part 2.

1. The missing fundamental

A pitched sound is a stack of partials at whole multiples of one frequency, the fundamental. Remove the fundamental and the pitch stays where it was: 200, 300 and 400 Hz together sound like 100 Hz, though nothing is playing at 100 Hz.

Why it works

The partials all repeat together once per period of the fundamental, so the waveform keeps the fundamental's period (the lower plot) with or without energy at that frequency. The auditory system tracks that periodicity. Schouten showed in 1940 that this residue pitch is not a distortion tone made in the ear: shift every partial by the same amount and the pitch moves, while a difference tone would stay put. Set the fundamental to 30 or 40 Hz and the partials you keep can sit entirely above what a small speaker reproduces, yet the pitch stays down at the fundamental.

2. Bass on a small speaker

A laptop or phone speaker can barely move air below about 150–200 Hz, and this bassline sits between 49 and 82 Hz. A virtual bass system removes the low fundamental and adds its harmonics instead, which the small speaker can play and your ear turns back into the low note.

Bass
Speaker

Small speaker runs everything through a steep filter below 180 Hz, so on headphones you hear roughly what a laptop plays. On a phone speaker, use Full range and let the real speaker do the cutting. Harmonics only plays partials 2 to 7 with the fundamental left out. Waveshaper bends the low band of the real bass through a nonlinear curve that creates harmonics, and those replace it. Real + harmonics keeps the real bass and adds the harmonics on top, which is how most bass enhancers work: full-range speakers still get the low note, small ones get the harmonics. The harmonics are set to sound about as loud as the real bass at a moderate volume (from the equal-loudness contours, since the ear is much less sensitive at 50 Hz than at 200 Hz). Use the Harmonics slider at the bottom of the page to match them by ear: switch between Real bass and Harmonics only on Full range until neither sounds louder.
