Drag rises with the cube of speed, so these numbers are only as good as the CdA you feed them, and CdA is the hardest thing on this page to know about yourself. If you have power-meter data, fit your own: hold a steady speed on a flat road in still air and solve backwards.

Why the real chase is usually shorter than this
  • Stoplights. Their moving average is not their clock average. A group that averages 18 mph on Strava spends real minutes stopped, and you close the gap for free at every red light you don't hit.
  • Regroups. Any ride with a no-drop clause waits at the top of a climb or at the turn. Time the chase to a known regroup point and the arithmetic below is a worst case.
  • Draft. The numbers here put you alone in the wind and them in a paceline. That is the whole cruelty of a late start: they are going faster than you at lower cost.
  • The first mile. Nobody rolls out of the parking lot at cruise speed. Your first two minutes are slower than the input; theirs were too.
The math

Head start — how far up the road they are the moment you clip in:

gap = their speed × (minutes late / 60)

You only close that gap at the difference in speeds, so:

time to catch = gap / (your speed − their speed)
distance to catch = your speed × time to catch

Which is why 1 mph faster is close to useless and 4 mph faster is a different ride: the denominator, not the numerator, does all the work.
