Soap film

A soap film is a sheet of water a few hundred nanometres thick, held between two layers of soap. Light reflecting off its front and back surfaces interferes, so every thickness reflects its own colour. Thinner than about 30 nm it reflects almost nothing and looks black.

The page runs a fluid simulation of the film on your GPU. Thick film is heavier than thin film, so it sinks along gravity (your phone's tilt, or the dial) and thin film rises. The frame drinks liquid from the film's edge, which sends thin plumes up from the border, and the film evaporates until black film spreads from the top and it pops. The colour of each point is the reflection summed over the visible spectrum for its thickness; the bar under the film shows that colour for every thickness.

After a pop, tap the empty frame or press Dip for a new film. Loop swaps the rectangular frame for a round wand, and the round dial beside the buttons turns gravity by hand.
