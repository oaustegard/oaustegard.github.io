Redesigning austegard.com on the Map of Austegarden

Written by Oskar Austegard · October 4, 2026

The old austegard.com was a bulleted list of links over a marbled background. Behind the text sat Oscar the Grouch, a 16×16 pixel favicon scaled up until it became a pun on my name. On a phone he landed on top of the links.

Earlier on October 4 Claude had redesigned muninn.austegard.com and I liked the result, so I asked for the same treatment here. I told it to use subagents in a workflow, which means several Claude instances working in parallel on separate parts of the job. Claude did the building. I chose directions and sent corrections.

Three directions

Three agents each built a prototype home page without seeing the others: a topographic map sheet, a pegboard workshop wall (a literal reading of the old site's “Grouch's Workshop” theme), and a typographic card catalogue with a search box. I picked the map, kept the catalogue's search, and kept a small pixel Oscar from the workshop wall, now at the bottom of the page and never behind text.

The real map

The map in that first prototype was fake. Its contour lines came from random noise and its coordinates were decoration. My reply: “I like the map concept, but if you're gonna use that it should be the actual map of Austegarden on Flatøy.”

Austegarden is a farm on the east shore of Flatøy, an island in Alver municipality, north of Bergen. Claude went to Kartverket, the Norwegian mapping authority, which publishes its data under an open licence (CC BY 4.0). It took contours from the terrain model, building outlines from the national building dataset, and roads, coast, lakes and forest from the 1:50,000 map data, then drew them as SVG in the site's colours. Kartverket's place-name register puts Austegarden within about 80 metres of the Google Maps link that has been on my home page for years.

To check the drawing, Claude rendered Kartverket's own topographic map of the same area and laid the two on top of each other. Coastline, roads and the farm buildings line up.

After it launched I told it the map should be background, not focus. Then I sent back a screenshot of the farm area with a circle drawn on it: “Move the map icon slightly south-west to the circled area.” The marker moved about 76 metres west and 55 metres south.

Search

The site has 84 small tools that run in the browser. An agent opened each one and wrote a one-line description from its README and code. The home page, the tools page and the section pages are now generated from those descriptions and the files on disk, so a new tool appears when its file is committed. I tried searching for “Muninn” and “agent” and got nothing back, so search now covers my links and blog posts as well as the tools.

Numbers

I measured with Lighthouse on a local copy, median of three runs. Mobile performance is 99 to 100 and desktop 100 on the home page, the tools page and the blog index. Accessibility is 100 on all three. The old site scored 81 to 91. The home page transfers about 200 KB. That covers the map and four fonts that now come from the site itself. One caveat: the machine that ran the tests blocks Google Fonts, so the old site looked lighter than it really was.

Two bugs

The strip of map along the top of inner pages was painting over the page titles. The usual accessibility scanner, axe, marked colour contrast as “incomplete” on every page because of the map, so it checked nothing. A script that samples the pixels behind each line of text found the problem. Separately, 16 of my 25 blog posts had a description made of their opening HTML with the tags stripped, such as “pClaude's Project feature…”. They now have real ones.

Still open

The map loads after the rest of the page, so for a moment you see plain paper. On screens wider than 2,800 pixels there is a faded strip of paper beside it. Search on the tools page needs JavaScript. The tool pages themselves are unchanged.
