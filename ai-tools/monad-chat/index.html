Monad Bullshitter

Pleias' Monad has 56 million parameters, less than half of GPT-2, and it reasons before it answers: it parses your question, rates its own confidence, ticks off a verification checklist, and then tells you the capital of France is the capital of France. It got 7 of 30 one-line fact questions right. Open the thinking trace; that is the show. It runs on your own GPU, in this tab, with 8-bit weights. The default weights carry a training-free edit that stops most of the repetition loops the original falls into; it does not make Monad know more. Switch to the original to compare.

backend … Weights are 65 MB, downloaded once and kept in your browser cache.
Source for Monad to answer from (optional, kept for every question until cleared)
Monad was trained to answer from sources it is handed (SYNTH's retrieval exercises). Paste text here and it is sent after each question, each paragraph in its own numbered <source_n> block as in SYNTH (up to ten); answers then quote it in <ref> tags, shown indented. Asked about a cat paragraph this way, both models got four of four questions right. Without it, both say a cat is not a mammal.
Settings: system prompt and sampling
Monad was trained on single questions with no system turn. In dry runs, a system prompt that described Monad made it talk about itself in 30 to 90% of answers to unrelated questions, and plain style instructions changed little. Without one it already says it is Monad.
Instructions inside the question are the form Monad was trained on. " Answer in at most three sentences." cut the median answer from 148 words to 62, though now and then the instruction becomes the topic.
Written into Monad's own turn, so it continues from it. Inside the thinking it steers the reasoning: planted "Cats are mammals ●." and both models reasoned to yes. With thinking skipped, Monad tends to fall into a multiple-choice format, and a planted "Yes," can be contradicted by the next words. "I’m unsure about the specific details" (a phrase from SYNTH's hedged answers) makes it decline and end the turn, on questions it would have got right as well as wrong ones.

About this page: Monad Bullshitter: a 56M reasoning model in the browser.

How the loop fix was found: Editing Monad's weights to stop its loops; the experiments are monad-weight-edit and monad-layer-parallel (the 8-bit build), and the system prompt was chosen in monad-chat.

Model: PleIAs/Monad, Apache-2.0, trained on SYNTH. Runs on onnxruntime-web 1.30 with WebGPU, or on the CPU where WebGPU is missing. Nothing you type leaves the tab. Do not believe a word it says.
