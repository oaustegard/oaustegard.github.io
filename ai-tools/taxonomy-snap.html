      What this is, and what it is not

      The taxonomy is the 860 distinct product_class values in Wayfair's WANDS dataset. Every label is embedded once, in the page; your text is embedded the same way and the nearest label by cosine wins. That is the entire classifier. It never emits a category that is not in the list, because it only ever picks from the list.

      It is a shortlister, not an oracle. It puts the gold category first for roughly 45% of held-out queries and in the top three for 59%. Queries whose wording has nothing in common with any label — gurney slade 56, a product name — have no reason to land anywhere near the right answer, and do not.

      468 queries, one gold label each. The second row is what a server round-trip buys, and the page can score it: the written labels are baked in, produced offline by gemini-3.5-flash-lite. The third row is the honest ceiling — when the vocabulary fits in a prompt, sending it wins by a wide margin. This approach is for the case where it does not: a provider enum cap, a five-thousand-label vocabulary, or per-call cost at volume.

      Running a small model locally to write the label instead does not work. Pleias Monad (57M) and Baguettotron (321M) score 0.425 and 0.400 — below the encoder's own baseline — because they echo the query or drift into invented wording. As likelihood rerankers over this page's top ten they score 0.325 and 0.350, while the right answer sits in that top ten 82.5% of the time. What a remote model contributes here is a prior over how taxonomies name things, and that is the first thing a shrinking model loses.
